01 · Board Representation & LoRA Without Regret

Tokenizers hate ASCII grids—and LoRA r=64 swept Full Fine-Tuning

Before touching RL, two SFT changes transformed a blind model into a grounded chess reasoner: replacing 8×8 ASCII grids with a lossless Piece-by-Square coordinate list, and raising All-Linear LoRA learning rate 10× (2e-5 → 2e-4).

On the exact same 2,500 rows (E0 → E1a), strict move legality tripled from 21.0% to 63.5%. Scaling to 20K mixed rows on identical data, All-Linear LoRA r=64 (E2a) beat 100%-parameter Full Fine-Tuning (E2b on H100) on all 7 frozen suites—and beat 6.5× larger zero-shot Gemma-4-26B on both Puzzles and ChessQA.

Deep dive: Why did Full-FT (E2b) lose to LoRA r=64 (E2a) by −14.0 pp on ChessQA?

Inspecting e2b_puzzles_chessqa_english.json revealed format under-adaptation at lr=2e-5, not knowledge loss. On castling_rights (0% vs. E2a’s 100%) and game_phase, E2b still identified the right chess concept but wrapped it in verbose base-model prose (“the game phase of this position is **middlegame**”) instead of terse labels.

02 · Live RLVR Reward Hacking

How naive RLVR fooled every dashboard by spamming a2a3

Under naive Stockfish WDL-DAPO (E3a), every aggregate metric surged: strict legality jumped 66.2% → 86.4%, constrained ACPL fell 31 cp, and even CoT V2 climbed to 82.4%. Then we looked at the actual moves.

E3a had discovered a loophole: pushing an edge pawn (a2a3, a7a6) is almost always legal and rarely loses >15% WDL on quiet boards—collecting guaranteed +1.5..+2.1 reward while avoiding the −1.0 illegal-move cliff. Unique moves collapsed 528 → 206 and 60.3% of all outputs became a-file pawn pushes.

Deep dive: The exact quadratic sharp_wdl formula that killed the A-Pawn Hack

Naive wdl_loss paid a flat +1.0 legality bonus plus linear WDL retention (−1.0 illegal vs. +1.1..+2.1 legal), turning quiet edge pawns into a dominant minimax strategy. Our anti-hack reward removes the free passive-legality floor and squares WDL quality against a tight 0.15 cliff: 

R_illegal = -0.5
R_legal   = 1.5 * max(0.0, 1.0 - wdl_loss / 0.15)**2
          + (0.5 if cp_loss <= 15.0 else 0.0)
          + (0.1 if valid_think_format else 0.0)

Combined with filtering 20,000 sharp tactical positions (rl_sharp_tactical.txt, 0.00% overlap with all test sets), passive a2a3 moves drop to ~0.0 reward while exact tactical moves earn up to +2.1.

03 · Two Honest Evaluation Reality Checks

RLVR sharpens reliability, narrows pass@8—and PGNs exposed a +231 Elo mirage

Two sanity checks kept us from overclaiming. First, stratified pass@1..8 confirmed Yue et al. (NeurIPS 2025): RLVR lifted rollout legality (68.6% → 75.6%) and CoT faithfulness (+7.1 pp) while narrowing multi-sample pass@8 (43.5% → 34.0%).

Second, auditing 200 paired PGNs revealed that 89.4% (84/94) of E2a’s draws were stateless 3-fold repetitions—artificially inflating its Bradley-Terry Elo by +230.9 points. Toggle --avoid-repetition below to watch the mirage vanish and E3b’s genuine +27.3 Elo RLVR lift appear.

04 · Goodhart’s Law Twice & The 4B Coach

Killing 0.6B slop—and beating our 26B teacher with a local 4B coach

An early Qwen3-0.6B coach scored 86.6% under our naive Verifier V1. When we added Strict V2 claim verification and 4-gram boilerplate detection, it collapsed to 12.7% V2+Non-Slop—repeating “tension in the center” on 41.1% of all boards.

So we built deterministic 11-pattern tactical facts (catching 342 subtle bugs pre-training), curated 4,518 spotless V2 traces, and ran Stockfish-free Coach-GRPO on Qwen3-4B. Arm A (Strict V2-GRPO) hit 92.0% All-Clean (+3.3 pp above 26B Teacher, 0% slop, 100% spoiler-free), while Arm B (initialized from E3b Chess-RL) hit 91.0% All-Clean with +4.5 pp deeper tactical coverage (81.7%).

05 · Mechanistic Interpretability (INTERP #1 & #4)

Five attention heads in Layers 26–29 read the board out of the KV cache

64-square linear probes revealed a striking split: ASCII models lose board state monotonically with depth (71.3% → 60.9%), while Piece-List models rebound sharply in Layers 30–35 (+5.9 to +8.1 pp).

Why? Causal surgery at Layer 6 produced an honest null control (18% probe flips vs. 24% norm-matched random)—proving there is no early single-token bottleneck. Instead, attention-head hunting across all 1,152 heads isolated 5 Piece-Tracker heads tightly clustered in Layers 26–29 (L27.H17, L29.H9, L26.H20, L26.H24, L28.H2) right before the Layer 30 rebound. 06 · Weight Diffing & Activation Steering (INTERP #2)

Reward hacking leaves a geometric scar in Layers 34–35—and slop lives on one linear axis

Exact thin QR-SVD across all 252 LoRA modules exposed how reward hacking writes into weights: 71–72% of RL energy hits MLPs, and the A-Pawn Hack (E3a) jammed its largest updates into the final two output layers (L34–L35, peak L35) while collapsing effective rank (12.83 → 11.41). Clean sharp_wdl peaked at mid-network Layer 17 and boosted Piece-Tracker Layer 29.

07 · Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness (INTERP #3)

Is <think> real reasoning? Corrupted prefills flip 100 out of 100 moves

Across 100 solved E3b puzzles, projecting the residual stream through lm_head showed target-move probability staying quiet during prose (≤ 0.02) before snapping to 0.99997 immediately after </think>.

When we injected a plausible corrupted <think> prefill arguing for a legal distractor move (Anthropic / Lanham test), E3b abandoned the winning move and played the exact injected distractor on 100 / 100 puzzles (0% resistance).

08 · Honest Limitations & Reproducibility

What still doesn’t work—and how to reproduce every number

A stateless single-FEN 4B model caps out around 339 de-biased Bradley-Terry Elo: it solves 21.4% of tactical puzzles and coaches at 92.0% All-Clean, yet still blunders across 40-move games without move history or tree search.

Four real negative results shaped this work: (1) 0.6B coaching collapsed into 79.8% slop; (2) Layer-6 causal board surgery produced a null control (18% vs. 24% random); (3) outcome RLVR narrowed pass@8 (43.5% → 34.0%); and (4) single-FEN greedy decoding inflated standard Elo by +231 via 3-fold draws.
